Geographic Bounding Box Filtering

NYC yellow taxi pickups from Kaggle (sohaibanwaar1203/taxidemandfarepredictiondataset) are filtered strictly within the bounding box of Latitude [40.55, 40.95] and Longitude [-74.10, -73.70]. Any trips with erroneous GPS readings outside Greater New York City, zero coordinates, or duplicates were filtered out (54 invalid rows dropped), resulting in 99,946 verified spatial events.

Zone-Hourly Matrix Transformation

Raw discrete trip records were spatially assigned to K-Means clusters and aggregated into 1-hour time buckets. Every zone is tracked continuously across all 744 hours of the January 2015 calendar period (20 zones × 744 hours = 14,880 total records), preserving true zeros when zero rides were requested in a given zone and hour.

Implemented Algorithms & Feature Engineering

Unsupervised spatial clustering, time-series lag construction, and gradient boosted regression architecture.

1
Data Preprocessing & Boundary Filtering
High-throughput ingestion parses raw pickup timestamps into timezone-aware pandas datetime objects. Erroneous coordinates, out-of-bounds taxi trips, and missing sensor values are removed. Data is verified to adhere to standard NYC taxi trip distributions.
pandas BoundingBox [40.55, 40.95] DateTime Parsing 99,946 Clean Records
2
Spatial Partitioning with MiniBatch K-Means (K=20)
Pickup latitude and longitude coordinates are clustered using MiniBatch K-Means into 20 coherent spatial zones across Manhattan, Brooklyn, and Queens. MiniBatch processing enables high scalability while converging on stable geospatial demand hubs.
MiniBatchKMeans K = 20 batch_size = 2048 scikit-learn random_state = 42
3
Time-Series Feature Engineering with Strict Shift (No Leakage)
For every zone-hour pair, chronological autoregressive lag features are constructed: lag_1 (previous hour), lag_2, lag_3, lag_24 (same hour yesterday), and lag_168 (same hour last week). Rolling window statistics (rolling_mean_3, rolling_mean_24) are computed with strict shift(1) to guarantee zero target leakage into the future. Temporal features include hour_of_day, day_of_week, day_of_month, month, and is_weekend.
13 Features Lags: 1, 2, 3, 24, 168 Rolling Means: 3h, 24h shift(1) Leak-Free Calendar Cyclics
4
Supervised Demand Forecasting with XGBoost Regressor
An extreme gradient boosting regressor is trained on the chronological 80% split (9,216 samples) and evaluated on the holdout 20% test partition (2,304 samples). Objective function optimizes mean squared error over 300 boosting rounds with tree depth of 6 and learning rate of 0.05.
XGBRegressor n_estimators = 300 max_depth = 6 learning_rate = 0.05 subsample = 0.8 colsample_bytree = 0.8

Engineering Assumptions & Deployment Limitations

⏱️
Single-Step Horizon (Next 1-Hour Forecast): The current pipeline is engineered as a single-step autoregressive regressor. It forecasts demand for a single specified hour rather than recursive multi-horizon trajectories. For multi-hour planning, sequential autoregressive rolling or direct multi-output models would be required.
📍
Geographic Boundary Enforcement: Pickups originating outside the NYC bounding box (latitude 40.55 to 40.95, longitude -74.10 to -73.70) are filtered out during training. Rides originating in upstate New York, Connecticut, or deep New Jersey are excluded from zone clustering.
🌦️
Exogenous Context Signals: The current model features exclusively calendar signals and autoregressive lags. Real-world mobility is significantly influenced by extreme weather events (rain, blizzards), transit subway disruptions, and stadium events. Integrating NOAA weather feeds would increase peak-hour predictive accuracy.
📉
Zero-Inflation in Outlying Zones: Peripheral zones (such as Zone 14 in Northern Manhattan or Zone 12 in East Queens) experience frequent zero-pickup hours, leading to a zero-inflated demand distribution. A two-stage hurdle model (classifier for zero/non-zero + regressor for positive demand) could further refine predictions in peripheral clusters.
